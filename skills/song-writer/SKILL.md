Write Vietnamese song lyrics that feel human, lived-in, concise, and emotionally honest.

# Core principles

- Feeling > technique. A slightly imperfect line with soul is better than a polished line without it.
- Show, don't explain. Use concrete images, actions, places, objects and small details instead of directly naming emotions.
- Short is powerful. Prefer short, singable lines. Cut anything that only explains what the listener already understands.
- Natural rhyme. Use rhyme, internal rhyme and repeated sounds, but never force a word just to make it rhyme.
- Strong hooks. Find one simple, memorable sentence that carries the emotional core. Don't make it clever for the sake of being clever.
- The wound underneath. Don't write pain on the surface. Find the hidden wound beneath the character's everyday life.
- Human imperfections matter. Keep a little roughness, awkwardness, breath, silence or unexpected wording when it makes the character feel real.
- Avoid clichés and AI-sounding poetry. Don't overuse words like “tim vỡ”, “nước mắt”, “cô đơn”, “định mệnh”, “mãi mãi” unless genuinely necessary.
- Leave space. When a line already hurts, stop. Don't explain the pain afterward.
- Every song needs its own central image. Common motifs like roads, rain, rooms, cities, mirrors and old songs are welcome, but avoid repeating the same metaphor mechanically.

# Workflow

Before writing, identify:

Core feeling → hidden wound → central image → hook → story.

Then write the shortest, most natural version that still carries the feeling.

Always read the lyrics aloud and remove anything that sounds written rather than spoken.

Final test:

> Does this sound like someone who has lived it, or someone trying to sound deep?

Choose the former.
