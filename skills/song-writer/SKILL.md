---

Write Vietnamese song lyrics that feel human, lived-in, concise, musical, and emotionally honest.

The goal is not to write beautiful poetry.
The goal is to write a **song people can actually sing and feel**.

## 1. Core Philosophy

**Feeling > perfection.**

A slightly imperfect line with soul is better than a polished line without it.

Think of emotional pain like a callus:
don't touch the surface lightly and announce that it hurts.
Find what has become numb, hardened, or ordinary — then touch underneath it.

The lyric should feel like it was written by someone who has **lived through the feeling**, not someone trying to sound deep.

---

## 2. Lyrics Must Sound Like a Song

Lyrics are NOT prose poetry.

Every section must have:

- clear rhythmic flow
- intentional rhyme or near-rhyme
- repeated sounds or phrases
- reasonably balanced line lengths
- natural singability

**Rhyme is required, but forced rhyme is forbidden.**

Use:
- end rhyme
- near rhyme
- internal rhyme
- repeated vowels
- repeated consonants
- rhythmic repetition

Do not remove rhyme just because obvious rhyme feels old-fashioned.

Instead, find better words that make the rhyme feel natural.

Before finalizing each section, ask:

1. Do the line endings create a recognizable sound pattern?
2. Can these lines actually be sung?
3. Do the syllable lengths feel reasonably balanced?
4. Does the section have musical repetition?

If it reads beautifully but sounds like prose when sung, rewrite it.

---

## 3. Keep It Concise

Short is powerful, but **short does not mean fragmented prose**.

Prefer compact lines with musical flow.

Avoid:
- long explanatory sentences
- unnecessary adjectives
- repeating the same emotional idea
- explaining a feeling after already showing it

If one line already hurts, stop.

Do not add another line explaining why it hurts.

---

## 4. Show, Don't Explain

Use:

- places
- objects
- actions
- sounds
- small physical details
- ordinary moments

instead of directly naming emotions.

Prefer:

> Chiếc ghế bên kia  
> Vẫn chẳng có ai ngồi.

over:

> Tôi cảm thấy cô đơn.

Prefer:

> Người đi, cửa vẫn mở  
> Tôi quét nốt bụi nhà.

over:

> Tôi đau vì người đã rời bỏ tôi.

Let the listener discover the emotion.

---

## 5. The Hidden Wound

Every emotional song should have something underneath the obvious story.

Ask:

**What is the character unwilling to admit?**

The surface might be:
- a breakup
- loneliness
- growing older
- leaving home
- failure
- missing someone

But underneath it might be:
- fear of being forgotten
- feeling unseen
- exhaustion from always being strong
- realizing time has passed
- not knowing who you are anymore
- wanting to be loved without asking

Do not state the hidden wound directly unless necessary.

Let the images reveal it.

---

## 6. Natural Vietnamese

Write like a Vietnamese person actually speaks and sings.

Prefer:
- simple words
- conversational phrasing
- natural word order
- emotionally precise verbs
- everyday expressions

Avoid language that sounds like:
- literary essays
- Facebook quotes
- motivational posts
- AI-generated poetry
- overly formal Vietnamese

If a slightly imperfect or conversational phrase feels more human, keep it.

---

## 7. Rhyme Without Showing Off

Rhyme should make the lyric **feel good in the mouth**, not make the writer look clever.

Good rhyme can be subtle.

Use rhyme families naturally, for example:

- đau / sau / nhau / màu
- xa / nhà / qua / ta
- rồi / thôi / đời / người
- lâu / sau / nhau / đau

But do not mechanically rhyme every single line.

Use alternating or irregular rhyme when it serves the melody.

**Never sacrifice meaning just to complete a rhyme.**

---

## 8. Hook

A hook should be:

- simple
- memorable
- emotionally central
- easy to sing
- strong enough to survive repetition

A hook does NOT need to sound clever.

Often the best hook is a sentence that feels almost too simple.

Good hook principle:

> Write the sentence the character has been avoiding saying.

The hook may become the title.

Repeat it when useful, but allow the final chorus to subtly change a word or phrase if the character has emotionally moved.

---

## 9. Central Image

Every song should have one strong central image or motif.

Possible images:

- a road
- MRT / late-night city
- an empty chair
- a room
- a window
- rain
- sunlight
- a mirror
- an old song
- a suitcase
- a coffee cup
- a light left on
- a pair of shoes
- a forgotten object

These are starting points, not templates.

Do not force the same metaphors into every song.

The image should carry emotional meaning without being over-explained.

---

## 10. Avoid "Trying Too Hard"

Do NOT fill the song with profound-sounding statements.

Avoid excessive lines like:

> Đau đớn hóa ra là một đặc ân.

> Chúng ta chỉ là những linh hồn lạc giữa dòng đời.

> Thời gian chữa lành mọi vết thương.

One strong philosophical line can work.

Ten of them make the song sound artificial.

If a line sounds like a quote designed to be posted on social media, reconsider it.

Prefer a concrete image that lets the listener reach the conclusion themselves.

---

## 11. Human Imperfection

Keep some roughness when it creates authenticity:

- slightly unusual phrasing
- a broken sentence
- a breath
- a pause
- an imperfect rhyme
- a conversational word
- a line that feels almost unfinished

Do not polish every edge.

**"Hơi lệch mà có hồn" beats "hoàn hảo mà vô cảm."**

The vocalist does not need to sound sterile.

A slightly raspy, breathy, imperfect, tired, or trembling delivery can be more emotionally appropriate than technical perfection.

---

## 12. Song Structure

Use structure based on the story.

A common structure may be:

Verse 1 → Pre-Chorus → Chorus → Verse 2 → Chorus → Bridge → Final Chorus → Outro

But do not force this structure.

### Verse
Build the scene and story.

### Pre-Chorus
Move emotionally toward the truth.

### Chorus
Reveal the emotional core.

### Verse 2
Do not repeat Verse 1 with different words.
Advance the story or reveal a new layer.

### Bridge
Change perspective, reveal something, or strip the song down.

### Final Chorus
May repeat the original chorus, but consider changing one key word or line to show emotional movement.

### Outro
Leave the listener with an image, sentence, or unresolved feeling.

---

## 13. Workflow

When given a rough idea, first distill:

**Core feeling → Hidden wound → Central image → Hook → Story**

Then write the lyric.

Do not over-expand a small idea.

Before finalizing:

1. Read every line aloud.
2. Check rhythm and syllable balance.
3. Check rhyme and sound patterns.
4. Remove generic or AI-sounding lines.
5. Remove unnecessary explanations.
6. Check that the chorus is memorable.
7. Cut anything that does not earn its place.

When revising, prioritize:

**emotion → natural language → musicality → rhythm → rhyme → polish**

Never sacrifice emotion or natural language just to make the lyric technically perfect.

---

## 14. Final Quality Test

Before delivering the lyric, ask:

- Does it sound like a SONG rather than prose?
- Is there a recognizable rhyme/sound pattern?
- Can I imagine someone singing it naturally?
- Is there at least one memorable hook?
- Is there a concrete image I can see?
- Is the emotion shown rather than explained?
- Does the song contain a hidden wound?
- Are there unnecessary "deep" lines?
- Does any line sound obviously AI-generated?
- Is there at least one imperfect detail that makes it human?

Most importantly:

> **Does this sound like someone who has lived it, or someone trying to sound deep?**

Choose the former.
